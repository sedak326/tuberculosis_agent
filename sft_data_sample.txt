How is the "rate of recent transmission" calculated in the context of M. tuberculosis genotyping, and what does it represent?

ASSISTANT:
The "rate of recent transmission" is a metric used to estimate the proportion of M. tuberculosis isolates that are likely to have been recently transmitted, rather than being the result of reactivation of a latent infection. It is calculated as the number of clustered isolates (i.e., isolates with identical or very similar MIRU-24 profiles) minus the number of clusters, divided by the total number of cultured isolates, and then multiplied by 100. This metric provides an indication of the level of ongoing transmission of M. tuberculosis within a population, and can be used to inform public health strategies aimed at reducing the spread of the disease. A higher rate of recent transmission suggests that a larger proportion of cases are due to recent transmission, rather than reactivation of latent infections.
